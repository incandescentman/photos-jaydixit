#+DESCRIPTION: Routes, shared data, interaction contracts, and maintenance notes for the red-carpet travel concepts

* Purpose

The Red Carpets concept lab presents Jay Dixit's red-carpet photography across six destinations: Busan, Locarno, Stockholm, Park City, Austin, and Toronto. It is a comparison environment, not yet a replacement for the original editorial journey.

The comparison gallery lives at ~/red-carpets/variants/~. The production domain remains unchanged while the work is reviewed through Vercel Preview deployments from ~codex/around-the-world~.

The Editorial Journey opening replaces its plain city-link list with a compact split-flap assignment board adapted from Departures. Its six rows remain navigation links to the existing city chapters; they do not open duplicate portrait panels.

* Shared Content

- [[file:../src/data/worldTourVariants.ts][worldTourVariants.ts]] is the shared registry for concept order, destination metadata, portraits, placeholders, and the Toronto archive.
- [[file:../src/components/WorldTourVariantNav.astro][WorldTourVariantNav.astro]] provides the compact cross-concept switcher.
- Busan and Locarno currently use clearly labeled temporary selections from Jay's real portfolio. They must not be described as photographs from those festivals until Jay supplies and approves the correct images.
- Toronto currently contains 13 verified TIFF portraits. Copy and tests should describe the actual archive size rather than the originally imagined 30-photo sequence.

* Map Fidelity

The Route's closing map uses [[file:../src/data/worldOutline.ts][worldOutline.ts]], the Natural Earth 110m land outline supplied with the original Route prototype. Its coordinate system is ~0 0 1000 386~.

Do not replace that outline with hand-drawn continent polygons. Visual changes should preserve the source geometry and be limited to styling, labeling, pins, arcs, spacing, and responsive presentation.

The current treatment uses a warm paper background, muted land, a fine atlas grid, dark labels, coral route arcs, and a highlighted Brooklyn home marker. A Playwright regression check protects the view box, high-detail outline, light background, and six destination markers.

* Typography

The Route uses the site's existing ~Newsreader~ family for its hero, city names, finale, route-manifest heading, and closing statement. Display text uses the family's real 500 weight rather than a synthesized ultra-bold weight. Small labels and route metadata use ~IBM Plex Mono~.

Do not restore ~Bodoni Moda~ without explicit design approval. Its extreme contrast and fashion-magazine proportions were rejected for this page.

Full Bleed preserves an explicit optical gap between each country label and its oversized city name. The display font's glyphs extend above their nominal line boxes, so a small ordinary margin can look overlapped even when the measured boxes do not intersect. Keep at least a 32px measured gap at desktop and mobile sizes.

* Navigation Vocabulary

The primary navigation uses *Red Carpets Around the World* for the canonical ~/red-carpets/~ route. Keep the full label: it makes the assignment-led travel feature explicit. The retired ~/around-the-world/~ route is intentionally not preserved or redirected.

The primary navigation labels ~/portraits/~ as *Portraits*. That route is a searchable person archive containing red-carpet subjects, Nobel figures, and other cultural or business portraits, so “Portraits” describes its full scope and distinguishes it from the assignment-led Red Carpets narrative.

~/portraits/~ is the canonical archive route, including person pages such as ~/portraits/lisa-gilroy/~. The former ~/red-carpet/~ index and person URLs remain HTTP 308 redirects for link and search continuity. Vercel enforces those redirects before serving the Astro site; the Astro redirect pages remain as a local and static-host fallback.

* Sequential Navigation Contract

- Full Bleed, Passport, Contact Sheet, The Route, and the original Editorial Journey provide optional controls that advance exactly one destination.
- Vertical destinations land below the fixed site navigation using a single ~scroll-margin-top~ offset. Do not combine root ~scroll-padding-top~ with the destination margin; stacking both offsets places a chapter too low.
- Contact Sheet advances horizontally on desktop and vertically after its mobile layout becomes a normal stacked sequence.
- Departures intentionally remains destination-driven: visitors choose a row rather than follow a prescribed next-stop order.
- Controls update the URL hash and preserve ordinary free scrolling.

* Motion and Accessibility

- Smooth navigation becomes immediate when ~prefers-reduced-motion: reduce~ is active.
- Toronto rapid sequences are capped at a readable rate and resolve into a static contact sheet.
- Reduced-motion visitors receive the completed Toronto grid without the rapid sequence.
- Navigation controls carry descriptive accessible names; image alternatives and temporary-image labels remain truthful.
