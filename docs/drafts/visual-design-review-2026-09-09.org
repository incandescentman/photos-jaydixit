* Recommendation

Your homepage has a visual identity worth building on: enormous faces, physical prints, warm paper, and elegant type. The strongest opportunity is to carry that level of art direction into browsing, then add an experience that could only belong to your photography: following a portrait from the press line into the public record.

I would start with the gallery presentation and exhibition viewer below. The Wikipedia journey is the most distinctive larger experiment. Proposals 1 and 2 are implemented in the local preview; the production site has not been changed.

* 1. Give the gallery pages the presence of a photographic exhibition

The live [[https://photos.jaydixit.com/gallery/][gallery directory]] uses small rounded cards, gray surroundings, and folder-derived labels such as Events → Nobel Prizes 2024. It feels much more ordinary than your homepage. On the [[https://photos.jaydixit.com/gallery/events/nobel-prizes-2024][Nobel archive]], filters occupy almost the entire first phone screen before any photograph appears.

Give each gallery a substantial cover photograph, a clean title, and the warm paper and thin rules already used on [[https://photos.jaydixit.com/before-and-after][Before & After]]. Let the photographs sit directly on the page, with restrained captions beneath them. On individual galleries, bring the first photograph much closer to the title; keep one compact visible filter row on desktop and put filters behind a Filter control on phones, with selected filters visible when active. Search stays immediately available.

The gallery directory should still help visitors choose a destination quickly. Use a consistent family of generous covers; reserve the strongest asymmetry and changing image scale for the galleries themselves. Your decision on September 9: clicking a gallery directory cover opens its gallery. The title remains another route into the same collection. Directory covers are explicitly exempt from the PhotoSwipe requirement; photographs within galleries open the viewer.

Why this is first: it improves the experience of photographs you already have, and closes the largest visual gap I saw. The tradeoff is fewer gallery covers in one screen. Keep the existing Packery engine and source organization; this is presentation work. Reference the finished [[https://photos.jaydixit.com/nobel-2024][Nobel editorial page]] for photographic scale and pacing.

* 2. Turn the lightbox into an exhibition viewer

Imagine clicking a print and seeing it centered on the same warm canvas, with a discreet strip of neighboring photographs underneath. You can jump directly to an expression or composition that catches your eye, expand a short contextual note, or return to the exact place you were browsing. On phones, the photograph keeps the space; thumbnails and context can be opened on demand.

PhotoSwipe, zoom, captions, and navigation already exist. The additions would be a thumbnail strip, useful links from the open photograph, and a consistent treatment across page types. A Story or Details panel could connect a portrait to its existing person page, event, Before & After comparison, or Commons source where those relationships are known. Copy attribution is available only when the source image contains embedded license or usage terms and an embedded creator. A catalog or person-level license alone cannot enable it.

This would make exploration feel continuous. Keep the largest possible image area, keyboard controls, touch navigation, and immediate dismissal; show the panel only when requested. There is no need for another viewer dependency: [[https://photoswipe.com/adding-ui-elements/][PhotoSwipe documents custom controls and navigation UI]]. The thumbnail strip would be a custom extension of that supported interface.

* 3. Show the portrait becoming part of Wikipedia

Add an optional publication view to [[https://photos.jaydixit.com/before-and-after][Before & After]]. A large portrait moves into a dated Wikipedia article capture; the visitor switches between the earlier and later appearance of the article. The payoff is seeing how your photograph changes the page where people encounter that person.

Keep the existing large photographic pairs as the main presentation. This additional view would explain the consequence of the replacement and give the page a visual moment particular to your work. Use actual, dated article captures or a clearly labeled reconstruction with verified source material. A historical replacement must not be presented as the current lead image without checking it.

There is also a smaller phone improvement worth testing: an optional Before / After toggle that alternates the two complete photographs in one viewing area. Currently they are stacked, so comparing expressions requires scrolling and remembering the first image. Preserve the stacked view as an alternative. Because these are different photographs taken at different times, a toggle is more coherent than a wipe that splices two unrelated faces together.

The tradeoff is additional publication evidence and presentation work. This is the strongest signature feature, rather than the fastest first change. A brief transition can reinforce the image's continuity; honor reduced-motion preferences and keep the static version complete. [[https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/view-transitions/][The View Transition API]] provides an existing mechanism to investigate for that optional motion.

* 4. Let visitors see your photographic choices

For a selected session, show a contact sheet of real neighboring frames, with the final selection enlarged. Clicking another frame brings it into the same viewing position; your own short explanation identifies what made the selected photograph work: the expression, gesture, background, or moment.

The [[https://photos.jaydixit.com/red-carpet/conan-obrien][Conan page]] already demonstrates how compelling contrasting gestures can be. That does not establish that its photographs are one consecutive burst; this feature would begin with a session whose actual sequence and selection you can verify.

This offers something a normal portfolio grid cannot: access to your eye. It also grows naturally from the physical-print language of the homepage. It needs your chosen frames and your explanation, so I would prototype one excellent example before creating a recurring format. Your rationale would come from you or an existing source, never from an invented account of what you were thinking.

* 5. Let a visitor assemble a personal contact sheet

An optional selection action could gather photographs into a private board. Opening the board would arrange the chosen images as a clean contact sheet; the visitor could change the order and copy a link to that selection. The visual reward is a collection they made while exploring your work.

This differs from the existing public likes: it helps someone remember and discuss a specific group of photographs. It could also support conversations with editors or prospective clients by attaching a selection to an inquiry. That is a proposed use, not a claim about a service you currently offer.

Keep selection controls quiet and separate from the main photo click. No account is necessary for a first version. This is useful if you want the portfolio to support active image selection; I rank it below the viewer and Wikipedia journey for pure visual impact.
