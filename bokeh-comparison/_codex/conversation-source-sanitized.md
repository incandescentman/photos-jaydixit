## Response:

**Yes. 75mm is essentially just as flattering to faces as 85mm.** The difference is quite subtle.

The important thing is that facial perspective is determined by **camera-to-subject distance, not focal length itself**\.

When you compose the same headshot with each lens\:

- **75mm\:** You'll stand slightly closer. The nose will appear very slightly more prominent relative to the ears.
- **85mm\:** You'll stand about 13% farther back. Facial features will appear marginally flatter, with slightly less emphasis on the nose.

Both focal lengths are excellent for flattering portraits. Most people would struggle to distinguish the perspective difference in actual photographs.

There's also an interesting point: if you photograph someone from the **same position** using both lenses, then crop the 75mm photograph to match the 85mm framing, the facial proportions will be identical.

## Which would I choose for you?

Given that you have plenty of room to use 85mm, I'd lean toward **85mm f/1.8**, particularly for headshots and beauty portraits.

But there's a compelling case for the Samyang 75mm f/1.8: it weighs only 230g, versus 371g for the Sony 85mm f/1.8. That's a substantial difference in a lens you might carry all day.

**I wouldn't choose 85mm because it's meaningfully more flattering. I'd choose it for the tighter framing and the particular portrait aesthetic you prefer.** If portability is paramount, 75mm is an excellent alternative. 📷
