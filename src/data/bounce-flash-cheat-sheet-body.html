Choosing bounce and modifiers

Using direct flash causes hard light.

Bounce flash is generally way more flattering.

In rough order of most flattering to least:

  1. Bounce off the ceiling or wall
  2. Rogue Flashbender
  3. Diffuser (e.g. diffuser dome)
  4. Flash pointed up with catchlight panel
  5. Direct flash with diffuser panel
  6. Direct flash

But yeah, bounce flash is nicer and more interesting on a person's face than direct flash.

So: get in the habit of always looking for white walls and ceilings to bounce flash off of.

Bouncing your flash off a white wall or ceiling already makes the light soft. For butterfly or Rembrandt bounce lighting with distinct shadows, leave the dome/diffuser off.

If you just want soft light, you can add a dome or diffuser. But know that it will scatter light around the room and fill in shadows.

Starting settings

When using TTL, start with no flash exposure compensation. Then change compensation as needed.

If you're indoors and don't want to deal with different color temperatures, use your flash as the main source of light.

For flash to be the main source of light, try ISO 100 and f8, with your shutter at sync speed. Set white balance to flash.

How to add ambient light

To add ambient lighting, reduce shutter speed. (Increasing ISO works too if in TTL. If in manual, to add ambient lighting you would have to decrease flash power at the same time as increasing ISO, by the same number of stops you raised ISO. Going from ISO 400 to 800 is one stop, so 1/4 power becomes 1/8.)
