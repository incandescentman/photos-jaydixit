			without paying you royalties.

<section className="wikiportraits-origin">

		<div className="wikiportraits-origin-lede">

			Sony pro camera and keep buying more lenses — even though the only photos I ever seemed to
			take were of my own friends at my own parties, and the only people who ever saw my photos were
			the people in the photos.
		</div>
		<div className="wikiportraits-origin-copy">
			<p>
				"If I could get you a press pass to shoot celebrities at SXSW would you want to do it?" she
				asked me.
			</p>
			<p>Um, yes? For a hobbyist photographer, it was a dream come true.</p>
			<p>
				I went to SXSW and took photos of Conan O'Brien on stage. My photos appeared on his
				Wikipedia page just days after I uploaded them. That was the start of WikiPortraits, and
				soon, Jenny made it official, founding a 501(c)(3) nonprofit with Wikipedia veteran Kevin
				Payravi.
			</p>
			<p>
				That was two years ago. Since then, I've traveled to film festivals and cultural events
				around the world, including Sundance, the Toronto Film Festival, Edinburgh Fringe, the
				International Festival of Journalism in Perugia, the Nobel Prizes in Stockholm and Oslo, the
				Locarno Film Festival in Switzerland, and the Busan Film Festival in Korea.
			</p>

<section className="wikiportraits-closing">
	<div className="wikiportraits-technique">
		The trick on a red carpet isn't getting a natural expression. These are professional actors —
		they always look good on camera. The trick is getting them to look at your lens when every
		photographer in the pit is shouting their name. I've tried wearing a red Switzerland flag hat
		and yelling "over here in the red hat!" I've called people by their character names. I got Ana
		de Armas's attention by saying, "Oye, Ana, por aqui, por favor!" She looked right at me and
		gave me the sweetest smile. Click, got the shot.
	</div>

        <div className="wikiportraits-mission">
	<div className="wikiportraits-mission-primary">
		But WikiPortraits isn't just about getting better photos. Because everything we shoot is
		freely licensed, our images are free for anyone to use, including news outlets that can't
		afford Getty or the wire services, from Brazil to Serbia to Japan. And most notable people of
		color still aren't represented on Wikipedia with a good portrait, or any portrait at all.
		Closing that gap is a big part of why the project exists. Wikipedia shapes how the world sees
		people. When the photo is bad, or missing, that's a problem worth fixing.
	</div>
	<div className="wikiportraits-mission-secondary">
